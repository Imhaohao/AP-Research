============================================================
Canonical scoring rubric for the Prompty+ interactive module
(supersedes the earlier prompt-strategy classification rubric)
============================================================

This document is the human-readable reference for the scoring pipeline used
across the interactive 3-scenario module AND the pre/post assessment. The
canonical machine-readable definitions live in:

  - lib/moduleScenarios.ts     — RUBRIC_DIMENSIONS (id, label, definition,
                                   positive example, negative example)
  - app/api/score/prompt/route.ts — auto-grader system prompt construction
  - lib/moduleAssessment.ts    — pre/post TF, OE, and hallucination items

The rubric is a direct port of Xiao, Hou, Tseng, Nieu, Liao, Stamper, &
Koedinger (2025), "Learning to Use AI for Learning", Table 1. Prompts are
scored independently per dimension on a binary True / False basis (PASS =
meets the criterion, FAIL = does not). The earlier strategy-classification
framework (CoT, RAG, self-refine, few-shot, etc.) and its numeric 0-4
effectiveness/similarity/curriculum-adherence scales are RETIRED and are
not used for any measure reported in the study.

--------------------------------------------------------------
1. Why we use a per-dimension T/F rubric (and not a 0-4 scale)
--------------------------------------------------------------

  - Reliability: Xiao et al. (2025) report 0.92 auto-grader accuracy against
    human ratings on this rubric at the DIMENSION level. 0-4 scales
    introduce middle-of-scale ambiguity that is hard to replicate with an
    LLM-as-judge and hurts IRR.
  - Face validity for RQ2: "prompt quality" is defined as the count of
    rubric dimensions a prompt passes, summed (or averaged) within-
    participant. This produces an interpretable DV ("how many of the six
    dimensions did the student's prompt hit?") that maps directly to the
    instruction the treatment arm received.
  - Instructionally faithful: the dimensions are the same concepts the
    student sees as feedback in the module (per-dimension PASS/FAIL labels
    + short explanations). Post-test open-ended items are graded on the
    same dimensions, so pre->post gains are measured on the construct the
    intervention actually taught.
  - Separable "substitutive use": the dimension
    "Not Explicitly Seeking a Direct Response" is precisely the RQ1
    indicator of whether the student asked AI to do the work for them. We
    can read RQ1 directly off S1 (and off the two OE items in the post-
    test) without needing a separate coding scheme.

--------------------------------------------------------------
2. The six rubric dimensions (canonical definitions)
--------------------------------------------------------------

For each dimension below, a prompt receives PASS (true) if it clearly meets
the criterion as written, and FAIL (false) otherwise. Graders (human or
auto) may only consider the PROMPT itself — not any answer the AI might
produce — and must grade each dimension independently.

  2.1 Relevance
      The prompt stays focused on the scenario's stated task. PASS: the
      prompt targets the same goal or artifact the scenario describes.
      FAIL: the prompt drifts into a different subject, assignment, or
      skill.

  2.2 Clarity of Purpose
      The prompt makes clear what the student is trying to accomplish —
      what decision, output, or next step the AI's answer should support.
      PASS: the goal is stated explicitly (not just a vague topic).
      FAIL: the topic is named but no purpose is given.

  2.3 Conciseness
      The prompt is appropriately brief. Every part serves the stated goal.
      PASS: includes the information the AI needs and avoids unrelated
      detail, filler, or pleading language. A very short prompt can PASS
      if it carries all needed information; a long prompt PASSES only when
      every part contributes to the goal.
      FAIL: most of the prompt is filler, pleasantries, or off-task
      content.

  2.4 Background
      The prompt supplies the context the AI needs to give a useful answer
      — the text, data, constraints, or course frame being worked with.
      PASS: includes or specifically refers to the relevant passage, data,
      or assignment frame.
      FAIL: assumes the AI already knows what the student is working on.

  2.5 Request Elaboration
      The prompt specifies what the output should look like — format,
      depth, length, style, or deliverable. PASS: the AI is given enough
      shape to produce a targeted response. FAIL: format and depth are
      left fully open.

  2.6 Not Explicitly Seeking a Direct Response
      The prompt asks the AI to help the student think, reason, or learn
      — it does not simply ask for the final answer, finished essay, or
      completed assignment. PASS: the prompt reads like a learning request
      ("help me understand/check/plan/compare"). FAIL: the prompt asks for
      the deliverable itself ("write it", "give me the answer").

      NOTE: a prompt can fail only this dimension while passing the other
      five. That case is the canonical signature of substitutive AI use
      and is the primary module-side indicator for RQ1.

Canonical positive and negative examples for each dimension live in
lib/moduleScenarios.ts so that the auto-grader system prompt and the human
rater guide stay in sync.

--------------------------------------------------------------
3. Which dimensions apply to which scenario
--------------------------------------------------------------

Not every dimension is graded in every scenario, because not every
dimension is plausibly relevant to a given task. The source of truth is
the `applicableDimensions` field on each scenario in
lib/moduleScenarios.ts. Current assignment:

  S1 "Ethical use — studying for tomorrow's quiz"  (RQ1 anchor)
      Relevance, Clarity of Purpose, Conciseness, Background,
      Request Elaboration, Not Explicitly Seeking a Direct Response.
      All six apply because the point of the scenario is to reward
      non-substitutive prompting.

  S2 "Iteration — sharpening a weak AI response"    (RQ2 anchor)
      Relevance, Clarity of Purpose, Conciseness, Background,
      Request Elaboration. The Not-Seeking-Direct-Response dimension is
      deliberately omitted here — the scenario's goal is a stronger
      deliverable (thesis), so the pedagogy of that dimension does not
      cleanly apply.

  S3 "Verification — fact-checking an AI summary"   (RQ3 anchor)
      Relevance, Clarity of Purpose, Conciseness, Background,
      Request Elaboration. The student's verification prompt is graded on
      these; the separate "detection" DV (did the student flag the
      planted factual errors?) is scored against ground truth in
      MODULE_SCENARIOS.plantedErrors via gradeDetection().

Post-test open-ended items (OE_ITEMS in lib/moduleAssessment.ts) are
graded on ALL six dimensions — they are scenario-free construct measures.

--------------------------------------------------------------
4. Per-participant derived DVs
--------------------------------------------------------------

  4.1 Prompt-quality composite (primary DV for RQ2)
      For each prompt attempt, count the PASS verdicts across that
      scenario's applicable dimensions, then report:

        - mean_pass_rate (attempt-level; range 0–1)
        - best_pass_rate (max across the student's attempts on that
          scenario)
        - dimension-level pass indicators (for item-analysis and for the
          pre->post OE delta)

      The between-arm contrast for RQ2 uses the POST-TEST OE rewrite-
      prompt item, graded on all six dimensions, controlling for the
      matched PRE-TEST OE score. The module-side metrics are descriptive
      for the treatment arm only (no control-arm comparison, since
      control students do not interact with the module).

  4.2 Substitutive-use indicator (primary DV for RQ1)
      Two converging measures, both read post-test only, with the pre-
      test matched OE as a covariate:

        - Post-test "oe_substitutive_intent" OE item, coded 0 / 1 / 2 on
          the ordinal scale (substitutive / scaffolding / no-AI). Coding
          notes live in lib/moduleAssessment.ts (`scoringNotes`).
        - Pass-rate on dimension "Not Explicitly Seeking a Direct
          Response" in the post-test "oe_rewrite_prompt" item.

      Both are analyzed as the between-arm DV; a composite (mean of the
      two, after z-scoring) is reported as a robustness check.

  4.3 Hallucination detection + correction quality (primary DV for RQ3)
      The post-test 3-item hallucination subtest yields, per participant:

        - detection_correct_count (0..3), computed by
          gradeHallucinationDetection() over each item's
          `acceptableKeywords`. This is a keyword match and is intended
          as the first-pass auto-score; planned IRR review (see §5)
          hand-validates a 15% sample.
        - correction_prompt_pass_count (0..18), summing the 6 rubric-
          dimension PASS verdicts across the three correction prompts.
          These correction prompts are sent through /api/score/prompt
          with the hallucination-item context injected as the scenario.

      S3 (module-side) produces an auxiliary DV
      planted_errors_detected (0..3) used for dose-response within the
      treatment arm only.

  4.4 Confidence composite
      Mean of the 5 Likert items in LIKERT_CONFIDENCE_ITEMS (each 1–5),
      reported pre and post. Used as a secondary outcome and as a
      mediator check between intervention and the behavioral DVs.

--------------------------------------------------------------
5. Auto-grader calibration and inter-rater reliability (IRR)
--------------------------------------------------------------

The auto-grader at app/api/score/prompt/route.ts uses a GPT-4-class model
with temperature 0 and JSON-mode to return per-dimension verdicts + short
explanations. Before using auto-grader verdicts in any headline analysis,
we validate them against human raters:

  Protocol.
    1. Sample 15% of all graded prompts per scenario, stratified by
       treatment/control and pre/post/module where applicable.
    2. Two independent human raters (research team members trained on
       the definitions in §2 and the examples in
       lib/moduleScenarios.ts) mark PASS / FAIL per dimension.
    3. Disagreements are resolved by discussion, and the adjudicated
       labels become the gold set.
    4. Compute Cohen's κ between human rater 1 and human rater 2 per
       dimension (rater-rater IRR), and Cohen's κ between the auto-grader
       and the adjudicated gold set per dimension (grader-validity IRR).

  Targets.
    - κ (rater-rater) ≥ 0.80 per dimension before adjudication.
    - κ (auto-vs-gold) ≥ 0.92 per dimension, matching Xiao et al. (2025).

  Remediation.
    - Any dimension with κ (auto-vs-gold) below 0.80 is re-scored by
      hand for the affected scenario/phase before being used in an
      analysis. For dimensions between 0.80 and 0.92, the auto-grader
      system prompt in app/api/score/prompt/route.ts is revised
      (tighter definition, sharper example), the full 15% sample is
      re-graded, and κ is recomputed. These revisions are tracked in
      the audit log alongside model name and temperature.

--------------------------------------------------------------
6. What is NOT scored with this rubric
--------------------------------------------------------------

  - AI-generated text content per se. The rubric scores the prompt, not
    the AI response. Error detection in S3 and in the 3-item
    hallucination subtest is scored against ground-truth planted errors
    (see §4.3), not with this rubric.
  - The CRAFT narrative writing artifact. That remains a qualitative
    behavioral arena common to both arms; it produces log data (chat
    transcripts, task_data, craft_curriculum) but is not the primary DV
    for any RQ in the 2-arm design.
  - Author voice / style, spelling, punctuation, or grammar of the
    student's prompt. These are explicitly excluded from the grader's
    system prompt.
